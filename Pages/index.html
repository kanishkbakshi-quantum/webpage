About Me

I am a Graduate Research Scholar at Tsinghua University working at the interface of quantum algorithms and quantum information theory. On the foundations side, I study encoding invariance of optimal quantum cloning — establishing how fundamental information-theoretic bounds behave under quantum error-correcting encodings — and I am increasingly drawn toward the intersection of quantum information and quantum gravity, where questions such as cloning in the presence of closed timelike curves probe how spacetime structure reshapes the rules of quantum information. Having worked on quantum algorithms, I recognized the need for novel, independent algorithmic primitives — and I am currently developing a novel quantum algorithmic framework that addresses the problem of error correction itself: embedding logical qubits in enlarged Hilbert spaces with dynamically engineered recovery manifolds, where autonomous dissipation steers errors back to the logical subspace without explicit syndrome measurement.

Beyond the lab: I am an amateur radio astronomer. I operate a personal 3-meter radio dish for pulsar detection currently I am trying to develop custom algorithm to isolate deep-space signals from PSR B0329+54 Pulsar.

Projects

Hybrid Graph Neural and Quantum Optimization for TrackML

Machine learning and quantum approaches for particle tracking at the HL-LHC.
This project explores scalable solutions for reconstructing particle trajectories from detector data, as posed in the CERN TrackML challenge. It covers the evolution from graph neural networks (GNN) for edge classification, to hybrid pipelines combining deep learning with classical physics-based methods, and finally to a quantum approach using Hamiltonian optimization and quantum annealing for global track assignment. The page includes technical explanations, open-source code, and a full project report.

Reliability Analysis of Dynamic Surface Code Deformation

Reliability Analysis of Dynamic Surface Code Deformation
This project investigates the reliability of quantum information encoded using the surface code, focusing on the impact of spatially correlated faults—such as those induced by ionizing radiation—on logical qubit preservation. The study compares static redundancy (high code distance) and dynamic code deformation (temporary reduction in code distance during bursts), quantifies logical error rates under burst faults, and provides design guidance for future quantum hardware using a rotated surface code lattice, fault injection, and performability metrics.
